You can safely only read articles marked as "Essential" and have a perfectly
good time programming robots. The "Useful", "Niche" and "Deep" tags are in
descending order of immediate importance, but I honestly recommend skimming all of them.
If you arn't particularly interested in learning about a concept, you should still
know the one sentence summary, so that when you come across a problem that requires them
you'll know where to look.

In reality most concepts in software development work like this. You learn just enough
about a lot of things, such that when you encounter a problem that can use them, you
know where to learn more. A problem you'll have as a self
taught <Info title="Self Taught?">With how fast tech moves, I think all developers
are self taught.</Info>, self directed developer
is "blind spots". You can go years and miss a concept that another developer considers
fundamental. Filling in these blind spots is a process that never stops.

My goal in these courses is to give you good fundamentals for the rest of your programming
journey.
