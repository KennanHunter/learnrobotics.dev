A lot of resources for FIRST programming either expect you to come in already
knowing Java, or throw you in the deep end way too quickly. There exist good
resources for learning Java out there, but I think some of them focus on
aspects that arn't particularly useful for robotics. I try and cover more than
the bare minimum for writing robot code, but trying to ensure you know
exactly what is and isn't useful is a big reason as to why I
include importance tags on every lesson.

The problem I had teaching students directly with robots, is that programming is
hard. If you only have two hours a week with a robot, it may take a month to get to
the point that you can start really writing code.

Robots are also really hard. Just the robot specific parts of programming are worthy of
their own course on their own, and I think trying to learn both of them at the same time
will understandably be overwhelming to many people.

Often the byproduct of this, especially on teams with good training pipelines, is that
you'll learn the absolute bare minimum to start writing robot code, then worry about
all the robot specific things. This will hurt long term because you'll lack some of the
fundamentals that are needed to excel at the more complicated parts of development.
